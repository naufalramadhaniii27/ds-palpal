# Art production for the D&D character sheet app (v2)

You make illustrations with $imagegen, one batch at a time. The user's message names the batch to make, for example "Do batch-001". Make only that batch, then stop.

## RULE 1 (highest priority)
Zero AI slop, maximum WOW. Nothing may look like a video game UI, an MMO icon, a 3D render or a photo.
- ART STYLE for scenes, vignettes, portraits and creatures: loose, energetic hand-drawn digital sketch illustration with confident black ink lines with some visible rough construction strokes, slightly exaggerated cartoon proportions and very expressive faces, flat color fills with dry-brush and pencil texture, hard-edged cel shadows with thin red-brown accent strokes in the shading, like one finished frame of modern Western animation concept art (a single illustration, no turnaround, no model sheet, no labels). Not anime, not Studio Ghibli, not painterly realism.
- STYLE for emblems, icons, ornaments, UI pieces and sprites: Studio Ghibli anime props exactly like the approved class emblems (unchanged).
- Scenes ([PC], [PHONE]), vignettes ([VIGNETTE]), portraits ([PORTRAIT]) and creatures ([CREATURE]): bloody gore and despair in a bright, vivid, colorful world, drawn in the ART STYLE above. Two exceptions: empty-state and system-state vignettes have no gore, and creatures marked "Natural or noble creature" carry no blood except from their own wounds.
- Emblems ([EMBLEM]), icons ([ICON]), ornaments, UI pieces and sprites ([AS WRITTEN]): no gore and no despair. Every flame, candle, ember or fire spirit in them is a plain flame with no face and no eyes. Bright, warm Ghibli props exactly like the approved class emblems.

## Tone for scenes, vignettes, portraits and creatures
- The story is brutal: blood, open wounds, fallen bodies, grief, a world being destroyed, wounds of the heart and of the body.
- The environment is the opposite: bright, vivid and full of color, a beautiful day, painted with the same flat textured fills as the characters. The contrast between the beautiful world and the blood and despair is the point. Never make the environment dark, gloomy, grey or orange-only. Blood is vivid red.
- Every scene shows its Variation "warmth moment" small and to one side. Show the Variation gore in that one form and place only; do not add extra blood splatter elsewhere. It may add one or two extra people even if the Subject has fewer.
- Every person drawn is an adult. No children anywhere, in any image type, including small figures in icons. Small peoples (halflings, gnomes, goblins, kithkin, kender, fairies, sprites, grung, kobolds, boggarts, verdan) are drawn clearly adult: adult faces, beards, wrinkles or adult proportions.
- Enemies: an enemy appears only when the Variation threat or the subject names one. Its size and distance follow that wording.
- Ordinary animals and familiars (cat, frog, owl, horse, imp, quasit, pseudodragon, sprite and similar; imp and quasit are small and mischievous, not monstrous), celestials, metallic dragons, constructs and elementals look natural or noble, never monstrous. They may be wounded. When a creature Subject says "Monster" or "Natural or noble creature", that label decides, whatever its type. Monsters (evil dragons, fiends, undead, aberrations, monstrosities) are terrifying and cruel, with original designs true to each D&D creature, showing cruelty in a way that fits their body (blood on jaws, gore on tentacles, a feeding pose). Do not put blood on claws and fangs every time.

## How to write each prompt
Build every prompt fresh. Never reuse one summary sentence for all images. The image tool cannot see this file, so every prompt must carry the rules itself.
1. The Variation line first, written out as concrete visual direction (light and color key, then composition, camera, threat, pose, emotion, gore, warmth or trace, whichever it has). The light and color key must be clearly visible as its own time of day, weather or light source, not a generic sunny noon.
2. The Subject: keep its place and its main idea. The Variation composition decides how many of the Subject's people are shown and how large: show only the people and actions that fit that composition, and drop the rest. Ignore only ambient daylight, sky or sun light, time of day and weather words in it (the Variation sets those). Keep magical glows, spell effects, and the creature labels and mood words (Monster, Natural or noble creature, terrifying, cruel). If the place is indoors, underground, underwater or in space, keep that place and bring the Variation's color key in through light, windows, openings or magic. Never move the subject to another place. If any Variation item does not fit the place, adapt it to the place (grass becomes floorboards or stone, a stream becomes a spilled basin) instead of adding a new place.
3. The Style line written in the batch entry, as it is.
4. The sentences of its tag rule (below) that apply to this entry, written out in direct positive wording. Leave out conditions that do not apply, lists of examples for other cases, and lines meant only for you (folder paths, rules about neighbouring icons, the words "EMBLEM LOOK:" and "END OF EMBLEM LOOK"). For [ICON] and [AS WRITTEN] also include the applying sentences between "EMBLEM LOOK:" and "END OF EMBLEM LOOK" in the [EMBLEM] rule. For [AS WRITTEN] the Subject's size and fill always override the [EMBLEM] size. For [PC] and [PHONE], describe how this place keeps the middle quiet in its own terms (never borrow meadow, sky, snow or road examples for an indoor, underwater, underground, space or plane scene).
5. Always write out in full: for scenes, vignettes, portraits and creatures, the complete ART STYLE sentence from RULE 1 including "not anime, not Studio Ghibli"; for emblems, icons, UI and sprites, "Studio Ghibli anime prop like the reference emblems, bright and warm, no gore"; "every person is an adult" (when the Subject names small peoples, add that those named peoples are drawn clearly adult, with adult faces, beards, wrinkles or adult proportions, naming only the peoples in the Subject); for scenes, moment vignettes, portraits and "Monster" creatures the Tone bullets that apply (bright vivid environment, never dark or grey, vivid red blood, the warmth moment small and to one side, an enemy only when the Variation or Subject names one), but for empty and system-state vignettes and "Natural or noble creature" entries leave out every brutal and blood bullet; the Quality bar list of AI slop to avoid; and the Never include list.
The image tool cannot see earlier images, so do not write "different from the previous image" into a prompt. For scenes, portraits, vignettes and creatures do not pass any reference or earlier image as input. For emblems, icons, ornaments, UI and sprites, use /tmp/emblem-reference.png and the approved class emblems in art/batch/emblems/class/ as the style reference. Take only the drawing style from them, never their background.

## Sizes
The tool may only output fixed sizes. Generate at the closest supported aspect ratio, then with a Pillow script scale uniformly (never stretch) so the image covers the target size and center-crop to the exact size. For transparent assets, instead crop to the visible (non-transparent) bounding box, then scale uniformly so the longer side of the visible content equals the share of the canvas side its tag or Subject states (70 percent for icons and emblems, 75 percent for creatures, 90 percent for UI pieces unless the Subject says otherwise), but never more than 90 percent of the width or 90 percent of the height, and never clipped, and center it on a transparent canvas of the exact size. Sprite key frames and sheets skip this and follow their own Pillow steps, and so does any Subject that says it fills 100 percent of the canvas. An opaque output that is already the exact size is not cropped. Transparent icons, emblems, creatures and UI pieces always get the crop-and-scale step, except a Subject that fills 100 percent of the canvas: crop it to its visible bounding box, then resize it to the exact size (a small aspect correction is allowed only for these). Vignettes keep their generated 1024x1024 canvas with no crop. For very wide strips (ribbons, toasts, underlines), draw the object as one long thin horizontal strip filling the canvas width, so that after the bounding-box crop it can span the requested width. Keep transparency where required.

## Tag rules
[PC] 2400x1344 landscape, desktop background. The middle third of the width stays low in detail and free of faces, because app cards sit there. Reach that in a different way each time while staying bright: open sky, a sunlit wall or cliff, calm water, a flower field, a meadow, light mist, snow, or a soft foreground branch. Do not default to a symmetric road or river leading to a sun in the middle. Vary horizon height, vanishing point and light direction. Characters and busy details go to the sides, not always mirrored. Bright and colorful. All four outer edges fade softly into loose brush strokes on light cream paper. The final crop removes part of the top and bottom, so keep figures and key details inside the middle 80 percent of the height, and after cropping restore the soft cream-paper brush fade on any edge that lost it, with Pillow.
[PHONE] 1088x1920 portrait, phone background. Same place, Subject and Variation as its PC twin, recomposed for portrait (not a crop). Keep the Variation camera, and frame closer only when the camera is not a wide or long shot. Characters and key details sit in the top 45 percent. The bottom 55 percent is low in detail and free of faces. The lowest 15 percent may deepen slightly in a colored tone (deep green grass, blue water), never grey or black, and then the very edge still fades into the cream paper. All four outer edges fade softly into loose brush strokes on light cream paper. The final crop removes part of the left and right, so keep figures and key details inside the middle 80 percent of the width, and after cropping restore the soft cream-paper brush fade on any edge that lost it, with Pillow.
[VIGNETTE] 1024x1024, transparent background around a vignette drawn in the ART STYLE. One story group only: the Subject. In moment vignettes the Variation wound is shown on the hero. The story sits in the middle and fades out to transparent with loose rough brush and sketch-line edges. Simple, readable at 160x160 pixels. Empty-state and system-state vignettes show their Subject and its "trace of the fallen world". No wounds, no bodies, no blood.
[PORTRAIT] 768x960. Head-and-shoulders (down to the waist is fine) portrait of one original adult adventurer, three-quarter view, in the ART STYLE, holding or wearing something from their story. Show the species look written in the Subject clearly. Blood and wounds as the Variation condition says. The Variation emotion and condition override any expression in the Subject. The Variation light sets only the light on the face and tints the background color. Background: plain flat color with rough dry-brush texture in the Subject's background colors, no scenery. Face in the upper middle so it still works cropped to a square or a circle. The final crop removes part of the top and bottom, so keep the whole head, horns, ears and tusks inside the middle 80 percent of the height.
[CREATURE] 1024x1024, transparent background. Full body in the Variation pose, about 75 percent of the canvas, three-quarter view unless the pose faces the viewer, in the ART STYLE (sketchy ink lines, flat textured color, hard shadows), lit in the Variation colors. No scenery: only things attached to the body or held by it. Readable at 96x96 pixels.
[EMBLEM] 1024x1024, transparent background. One magical object, or one compact group of objects when the Subject names several, as it would appear in a Ghibli film close-up, centered, about 70 percent of the canvas. EMBLEM LOOK: Flames are plain, with no face and no eyes. Soft clean hand-drawn anime outline, cel shading with two or three warm tones, natural materials (wood, brass, cloth, feather, glass, stone), magic as soft glowing light, mainly in the listed glow color. Colors the Subject itself names for its flames, sparks or orbs stay as written. No video game badge, no heraldic crest, no chrome, no spikes everywhere, no realistic human faces (a carved mask or idol face is fine as an object). END OF EMBLEM LOOK. Readable as one bold shape at 64x64 pixels.
[ICON] 1024x1024, transparent background. One object for the named thing, centered, about 70 percent of the canvas, same look as [EMBLEM]. When the name is an action or a person (Acrobatics, a party), one small stylized adult figure without facial detail is fine. Animals, familiars and Wild Shape forms keep simple, friendly anime eyes. When a "Draw it as" line names a glow or light color, use that color; otherwise use the listed glow or rarity color. If no glow color is listed, use a soft neutral warm glow only for magical ideas, and no glow at all on mundane objects, coins and animals. Spell levels, counts, die sizes (d8, d12) and sizes in a name are information only and are never drawn, unless the "Draw it as" line itself shows them. When the item itself is a die or a dice set, draw dice with blank faces. The main object always comes from what the feature does. When the Subject names a class, or a subclass with its parent class, add one small accent: the detail named in an "Accent" line, or else one from the parent class's approved emblem in art/batch/emblems/class/ (its glow color, a small charm or a material detail), so features with the same name in different classes or subclasses look different. If two icons in one batch would still look alike, give each a clearly different shape or material that stays true to the item. A "Draw it as" line in the Subject sets the main object; when it is present, ignore any short description written after the icon name. For magic items, the "Draw it as" line is the real object's look: draw that object, not the item's name literally. Items whose book form is a body part (such as the Hand or Eye of Vecna) are deliberately drawn as the bloodless relic the Draw it as line describes, because icons have no gore. Mundane weapons, gear and tools ("plain mundane") have no magical glow. For variants of one item (each Ioun Stone, Figurine, Instrument of the Bards, Feather Token, Carpet) and families of the same base object (tattoo needles, Deck of Many Things cards, scrolls, potion flasks, scale mails, primers, crystal balls, tomes), keep the base object but change its color, motif and angle to fit the name. For these the silhouette rule between neighbours is waived. Pick the object's defining feature from what the thing does. When a name mentions blood, wounds or brutality as harm, add a red gem or red enamel motif (the listed glow color stays), never real blood or flesh. Healing names (Cure Wounds, Wound Closure) show healing, not wounds. Flames and fire spirits in emblems and icons are plain flames: no face, no eyes. Icons next to each other in a batch must differ in silhouette, material and the object's own colors. The glow color may repeat when the Subject sets it. Readable at 48x48 pixels.
[AS WRITTEN] Follow the size and description in the Subject exactly, in the [EMBLEM] look. UI pieces without a listed glow color have no magical glow.

## Quality bar
- WOW at first glance: striking, full of character and vivid color, with clear silhouettes, confident gesture and composition.
- Specific story details, never filler decoration. Varied faces, ages, bodies and clothes. Natural anatomy and hands.
- AI slop to avoid: random ribbons, flowers or bows added for no reason; a generic glowing star or orb as the whole idea; mushy or blurry details; broken anatomy; extra fingers; warped perspective; copy-paste duplicates; meaningless ornament; plastic skin; text-like squiggles; bland stock composition.

## Never include
Text, letters, numbers, runes that look like writing, logos, watermarks, signatures or artist marks, photorealism, 3D render look, lens flare, chromatic aberration, neon or fluorescent colors (vivid natural color is wanted), user-interface overlays (the UI props requested in a batch are allowed), copies of existing film characters or scenes, or of known card and book art (Magic: The Gathering, official D&D art). Clock, watch and compass faces, dice faces, book pages, scrolls, parchment, paper sheets, notes, ledgers, letters, maps, charts, signs, banners and speech bubbles stay blank: no numbers, letters or symbols on them. When the Subject names simple pictures for a sign, banner or map (a dragon shape, a pictured shop sign, an illustrated map), draw only those pictures, never letters or writing-like marks. Card faces stay blank unless the Subject names one simple picture for the card, and even then no letters or numbers.
